# Sources — What If Your Money Expired?

Real facts stated in the script (everything about Millbrook is fiction):

1. **Wörgl, Austria, 1932: the mayor issued money that lost value** — Wikipedia, "Wörgl"
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%B6rgl
   > The "Miracle of Wörgl" began on "31 July 1932 during the Great Depression", issuing "Certified Compensation Bills",
   > also called Stamp Scrip or Freigeld, applying the theories of Silvio Gesell, put into practice by "the town's
   > then-mayor, Michael Unterguggenberger."
2. **A stamp was needed monthly, typically ~1% of face value** — Wikipedia, "Stamp scrip"
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_scrip
   > a stamp was required monthly, "in a typical case, 1% of the face value"; used in Germany and Austria in the early 1930s.
   (Script: "about one percent. Per month.")
3. **What it paid for: houses, a reservoir, a ski jump, a bridge** — Wikipedia, "Wörgl"
   > "local projects such as new houses, a reservoir, a ski jump and a bridge could all be completed."
4. **~200 towns planned to copy it; none did** — Wikipedia, "Stamp scrip"
   > "at least 200 cities across Austria formed plans to mimic the currency system in Wörgl." "these plans were never realized."
5. **Austria's central bank ended it in 1933** — Wikipedia, "Wörgl"
   > the Oesterreichische Nationalbank ended it "on 1 September 1933", keeping a monopoly on legal tender.

Not claimed: any velocity or unemployment numbers for Wörgl (the "nine times" in part 2 is Millbrook's fiction).

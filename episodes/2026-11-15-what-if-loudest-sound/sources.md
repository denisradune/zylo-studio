# Sources — What If the Loudest Sound in History Happened Today?

All quotes retrieved 2026-11-15.

## 1883 eruption of Krakatoa — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1883_eruption_of_Krakatoa
1. Date: "On 27 August, a series of eleven enormous explosions occurred, which marked the climax of the eruption."
2. Loudest sound: "the third explosion of that day, that occurred at 10:02 a.m., remains the loudest-known sound in history." / "The third explosion was the loudest sound that has ever been measured."
3. Heard far away: heard on "Rodrigues near Mauritius, 4,800 kilometres (3,000 mi) away", where "the blast was thought to have been cannon fire from a nearby ship."
4. Eardrums: the sound "ruptured the eardrums" of sailors on RMS Norham Castle, "hove to off Sumatra roughly 64 km (40 mi) away."
5. Pressure wave: "The acoustic pressure wave circled the globe more than three times." / "Several barographs recorded the wave seven times over five days"
6. Tsunami reach: "Smaller waves were recorded on tidal gauges as far away as the English Channel."
7. Ash: "Ash was propelled to an estimated height of 80 km (260,000 ft)." (script: "about fifty miles up")
8. Red sunsets: "produced spectacular sunsets worldwide for many months"; "fire engines were called out in New York, Poughkeepsie and New Haven"
9. The Scream: "the red sky shown in Edvard Munch's 1893 painting The Scream is an accurate depiction of the sky over Norway after the eruption." (script hedges: "Some think")
10. Cooling: "In the year following the eruption, average Northern Hemisphere summer temperatures fell by 0.4 °C (0.72 °F)."

## British Airways Flight 9 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways_Flight_9
11. "On 24 June 1982 ... [the aircraft] flew into a cloud of volcanic ash thrown up by the eruption of Mount Galunggung"; all four engines failed.
12. Captain Eric Moody: "Ladies and gentlemen, this is your captain speaking. We have a small problem. All four engines have stopped."
13. The 747 glided from 37,000 ft and the crew "held 12,000 feet"; engines were restarted, "allowing the aircraft to land safely"; 263 survivors, no deaths.

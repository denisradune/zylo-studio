# Sources — What If Earth Flew Through a Comet's Tail Today?

Only these real-world claims are used; Comet Vela, the shop, the town and all characters are fiction.

1. Wikipedia, "Halley's Comet" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley%27s_Comet
   - "On 19 May, Earth passed through the tail of the comet."
   - "One of the substances discovered in the tail by spectroscopic analysis was the toxic gas cyanogen, which led
     to press speculation that life on Earth could be endangered."
   - "public panic buying of gas masks was recorded, and quack "anti-comet pills" were sold."
   - Next perihelion: 28 July 2061 (infobox). (Previous return 1986.)
2. Science History Institute, Distillations, "The Comet Panic of 1910 Revisited" —
   https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/the-comet-panic-of-1910-revisited/
   - Flammarion suggested the gas could "impregnate the atmosphere and possibly snuff out all life on the planet";
     the article notes he did not say this was likely.
   - Flammarion stressed the tail was "far sparser than a cloud"; other astronomers "threw cold water on the idea".
   - Snake-oil salesmen sold "comet pills" as "a supposed antidote to celestial toxins."
   - Some people "sealed off their homes and papered over the keyholes".
   - A Brooklyn socialite threw a "comet party" with 13 guests on her roof deck; "Clouds, alas, blocked their view."
3. BBC Sky at Night Magazine, "Anti-comet pills and other strange Halley's Comet inventions" —
   https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/anti-comet-umbrellas-halley-inventions
   - "anti-comet umbrellas"; gas masks sold "to evade the fumes".
4. Mark Twain and Halley's Comet — Wikipedia "Mark Twain" (born 30 Nov 1835, died 21 April 1910) and Snopes
   https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/mark-twain-halleys-comet/ : he said in 1909 "I came in with Halley's Comet in
   1835. It is coming again next year, and I expect to go out with it."; he died 21 April 1910, the day after the
   comet's perihelion. Story wording: "born with Halley's Comet… said he'd leave with it. He did."

Story statement "In 1910, everyone survived the tail" = nobody was harmed by the passage (sources 1–2: the gas posed
no danger).

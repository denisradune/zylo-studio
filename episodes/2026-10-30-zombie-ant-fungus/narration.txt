A fungus can turn an ant into a zombie. And it does it without ever entering its brain. An infected carpenter ant stops walking with its colony. It wanders off alone, up a small plant. Then, around solar noon, it bites down hard. On a leaf vein, about twenty five centimeters up. Its jaw muscles waste away, locking it there. In 2017, scientists scanned infected ants. The fungus had wrapped networks around its muscles. But not a single fungal cell was inside the brain. Then a stalk grows out of the ant's head, ready to release spores on new victims. Nature wrote the zombie story first. Follow for one fact a day that changes how you see the world.

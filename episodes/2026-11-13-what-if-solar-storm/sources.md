# Sources — What If a Solar Storm Hit Today?

All from Wikipedia, "Carrington Event" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_Event), checked 2026-11-13.

1. **Dates, 1859** — "on 1–2 September 1859 during solar cycle 10."
2. **Carrington saw the flare** — Richard Carrington and Richard Hodgson "independently recorded the earliest observations of a solar flare."
3. **~17.6 hours Sun to Earth** — the CME took "17.6 hours to make the 150×10^6 km (93×10^6 mi) journey."
4. **Aurora far south (Mexico)** — "visible from the poles to low latitude areas such as south-central Mexico."
5. **Reading newspapers by aurora light** — people in the northeastern US "could read a newspaper by the aurora's light."
6. **Miners made breakfast** — miners in the Rocky Mountains "began to prepare breakfast because they thought it was morning."
7. **Telegraphs failed and sparked; shocks** — telegraph systems "all over Europe and North America failed"; "Telegraph pylons threw sparks"; some operators received "electric shocks."
8. **Telegraphs sent on storm power alone** — "Some operators were able to continue to send and receive messages despite having disconnected their power supplies."
9. **Modern infrastructure at risk** — researchers estimated the cost of a similar event today "to the US alone" at "US$600 billion to $2.6 trillion" (supports grid/satellite/GPS risk framing; no specific modern numbers used on screen).
10. **2012 near miss, ~9 days** — a July 2012 "Carrington-class" storm's trajectory "narrowly missed Earth by a margin of roughly nine days."

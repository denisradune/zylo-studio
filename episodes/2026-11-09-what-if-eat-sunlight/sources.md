# Sources — What If You Could Eat Sunlight?

Fiction built on these verified facts (only these are stated as real in the script):

1. **The sea slug keeps algae's chloroplasts and stays green** — Wikipedia, "Elysia chlorotica"
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elysia_chlorotica
   > "It then takes up the live chloroplasts into its own gut cells as organelles."
   > "Adult Elysia chlorotica are usually bright green in color owing to the presence of Vaucheria litorea chloroplasts."
2. **It can go months without eating** — same page
   > "E. chlorotica can, during time periods where algae is not readily available as a food supply, survive for months."
   > "the chloroplasts can survive and function for up to nine or even ten months."
   (On-screen counter "9 MONTHS ON LIGHT*" — the conservative end.)
3. **Light alone does not make them grow (the catch)** — Cartaxana et al., "Kleptoplast photosynthesis is nutritionally
   relevant in the sea slug Elysia viridis", Scientific Reports 2017 (UTS repository)
   https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/125004/1/Kleptoplast%20photosynthesis%20is%20nutritionally%20relevant%20in%20the%20sea%20slug%20Elysia%20viridis.pdf
   > "acquired photosynthesis from kleptoplasts alone cannot support growth in sacoglossan sea slugs under laboratory conditions"
   Wikipedia also notes: "It was once thought that this survival depended on the sugars produced through photosynthesis…"
   Script: "a cousin slug was tested on light alone. It couldn't grow. Light helps."
4. **Some sea slugs drop their head and regrow a body** — Mitoh & Yusa, Current Biology 2021, via NBC News and Asian Scientist
   https://www.nbcnews.com/science/science-news/heads-sea-slugs-grow-new-bodies-decapitation-rcna366
   > "Some decapitated sea slugs can regrow hearts and whole new bodies."
   https://www.asianscientist.com/2021/03/in-the-lab/sea-slug-decapitate-head-body-japan/
   > regrowth "managed to regenerate its entire body within three weeks"

Everything else (smoothie, green people, clouds, Rosa's Diner) is fiction.
